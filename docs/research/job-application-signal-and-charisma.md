# Job-application signal and charisma

Updated: 2026-07-14

## Conclusion

JHAA should not generate one universally "warm" application. It should choose a
channel and a message strategy from the opportunity's context, then express
competence through specific proof and personality through the way the message is
written. The strongest evidence supports:

- credible network paths and referrals as useful channels, without assuming that
  offline contact or any referral is automatically better than a direct application;
- employer-specific motivation and clear writing over generic professional polish;
- short examples and stories over unsupported self-description or a list of skills;
- an informal, high-personality format only when the employer, role, and application
  instructions make it appropriate;
- video as an optional experiment for suitable, communication-heavy contexts, not a
  default attachment;
- preserving the formal application path even when a parallel introduction or
  outreach path is available.

The research does **not** establish a universal "charisma" formula, that in-person is
always higher-signal than LinkedIn, or that video always improves an application.

## Evidence

### Channel: use credible access, not a fixed channel hierarchy

A five-year set of randomized LinkedIn experiments involving more than 20 million
people found that weak ties causally increased job mobility. The relationship was
non-linear and varied by industry: moderately weak ties were often most useful, while
stronger ties mattered more in less digital industries. This supports actively looking
for a credible introduction outside the candidate's closest circle; it does not show
that sending cold messages to strangers works, or that LinkedIn is inherently a
low-signal channel. [Rajkumar et al., *A Causal Test of the Strength of Weak
Ties*](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/causal-test-strength-weak-ties)

Referral evidence points in the same direction but is conditional. In one US
corporation, referred candidates were more likely to be hired and had longer tenure;
research across nine large firms likewise found referral advantages and evidence of
better job-specific fit. These are employer-side referral systems, not evidence that a
nominal referral obtained from an unknown employee carries the same signal.
[Brown, Setren, and Topa, *Do Informal Referrals Lead to Better
Matches?*](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr568.pdf),
[Burks et al., *The Value of Hiring through Employee
Referrals*](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qju041)

Referral systems can also reproduce unequal access when networks are segregated.
That is another reason to use referrals as one route, not as the definition of merit or
the only acceptable path. [Miller and Schmutte, *The Dynamics of Referral Hiring and
Racial Inequality*](https://research.upjohn.org/up_workingpapers/352/)

**JHAA implication:** produce a `channel hypothesis`, not a channel command. Rank a
real warm introduction, a relevant weak tie, a direct hiring-manager route, and the
formal application according to actual access and the employer's process. Explain why
the proposed route is credible, and never suppress the required formal application.

### Message: personal motivation carries more signal than generic polish

Two 2026 field experiments found that LLM assistance improved spelling, formality,
and standardized sections of cover letters but did not increase interview invitations.
The gains were concentrated in less influential generic sections, while recruiters
placed more weight on personal motivation and clarity. The study used ChatGPT 3.5 and
Dutch recruiters, so it is evidence against relying on generic AI polish, not against
all assisted drafting. [Abbas Nejad et al., *Labor Market Signals: The Role of Large
Language Models*](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/labor-market-signals-the-role-of-large-language-models-2/)

Employer practice also differs. For example, 37signals says it requires and reads
cover letters first and uses thoughtful effort to form its shortlist. This is strong
first-party guidance for an application to 37signals, but not a rule for employers that
screen primarily through structured forms or work samples. [37signals, *The Case for
Cover Letters*](https://37signals.com/podcast/the-case-for-cover-letters/)

**JHAA implication:** each draft needs one opportunity-specific reason for writing,
one proof point that answers the employer's actual need, and language that sounds like
the candidate. Removing stiffness is insufficient if the result is still interchangeable
with another applicant or company.

### Competence plus personality: demonstrate; do not self-label

A cross-national field experiment covering 13,162 fictitious applications found only
a small positive effect from written competence signals and no average effect from
written warmth self-descriptions; the competence effect was not robust to every model.
Statements such as "I am warm" or "I am a team player" are therefore a weak basis for
engineering charisma into application copy. [Veit et al., *The “Big Two” in Hiring
Discrimination*](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167220982900)

In a field study of real employment interviews, applicant tactics focused on the
interviewer or organization were associated with perceived fit, hiring
recommendations, and indirectly job offers, while self-promotion effects were weak and
generally non-significant. This supports genuine, specific attention to the employer
over a bragging inventory, though it should not be read as permission for empty
flattery. [Higgins and Judge, *The Effect of Applicant Influence Tactics on Recruiter
Perceptions of Fit and Hiring Recommendations*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15327349/)

Analysis of 62 real selection interviews found that stories and story-like answers to
past-behaviour questions increased hiring recommendations, while self-descriptions
decreased them. The sample is small and concerns interviews, but the result supports a
portable writing rule: show one compact situation, action, and consequence instead of
claiming an adjective. [Bangerter, Corvalan, and Cavin, *Storytelling in the Selection
Interview?*](https://libra.unine.ch/entities/publication/f119837d-06ab-4918-8eb5-72ff5b8e0fd5)

**JHAA implication:** operationalize "charisma" as observable writing choices: a
specific opening, conversational rhythm, curiosity about their problem, one vivid proof
moment, and confident understatement. Do not write "I am charismatic," stack
adjectives, or turn every proof point into a bullet list. Any humour or playfulness must
be licensed by the employer's own tone and the application context.

### Format: richer media can help, but only conditionally

A field experiment sending applications to 2,021 Québec employers found that an
invitation to view a professionally produced video résumé increased callbacks by more
than 10 percentage points. The experiment targeted secretary and receptionist roles
during a severe local labour shortage, so it supports video for comparable
communication-heavy contexts rather than a universal video-first strategy.
[Bellemare et al., *Video Resumes and Job Search Outcomes*](https://docs.iza.org/dp13656.pdf)

Richer media also adds cues without guaranteeing better judgment. In a study of 296
recruiters, video and audio résumés did not make recruiters' Big Five personality
judgments more accurate than paper résumés, apart from limited evidence around
extraversion. [Apers and Derous, *Are They Accurate? Recruiters' Personality
Judgments in Paper versus Video Resumes*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2017.02.063)

**JHAA implication:** recommend a short video only when the role rewards live
communication, the employer invites creativity or informal contact, the recipient is
likely to watch it, and the candidate can produce it naturally. Keep a concise written
message and normal application alongside it. Treat video as a testable channel choice,
not as proof of personality.

### Context changes what counts as a strong signal

In experiments comparing remote and onsite work settings, recruiters gave competence
more weight than warmth, especially for remote roles. The result is narrow, but it
directly rejects one fixed competence-to-warmth ratio for every application.
[Fousiani, Sypes, and Armenta, *Applying for Remote Jobs? You'd Better Be
Competent!*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2022.112063)

**JHAA implication:** classify the application context before drafting: structured or
informal, remote or relationship-heavy, technical or customer-facing, cold application
or existing conversation, and standard or creativity-inviting. That classification
should select the format and tone budget; it should not change the underlying facts.

## Recommended contract

Separate reusable job-search strategy from Giacomo's personal voice:

1. The career-system's advancement capability should own the generic decision logic:
   inspect application instructions, classify context, choose a channel hypothesis,
   select proof, draft, and keep real-world send/submit actions gated. This makes the
   behaviour available during manual use as well as automation runs.
2. The KB should own Giacomo's durable communication preferences and approved examples:
   warmer and more human than conventional corporate copy; smart and casual where the
   context permits; competence shown through understated proof; charisma expressed in
   the discourse rather than claimed; no cold skill inventory.
3. The kb-infra automation should resolve that KB context and invoke the sink's generic
   capability. It should not duplicate either the personal voice or the career-system's
   drafting algorithm in the materialized automation prompt.
4. Every generated pack should expose its reasoning in reviewable fields: context
   classification, proposed channel, recipient, opportunity-specific hook, selected
   proof point, tone choice, and why any richer medium is appropriate.
5. Track outcomes by context and channel. Research supplies priors; JHAA's own approved,
   sent, replied-to, and interview-converting packs should drive later tuning.

## Confidence and limits

Confidence is high that generic polish, unsupported warmth claims, and a fixed channel
hierarchy are poor design foundations. Confidence is medium that compact narrative,
fit-focused attention, and selective richer media will improve Giacomo's applications;
the most relevant studies span different stages, roles, countries, and labour markets.

No source reviewed establishes "charisma" as a single causal treatment across job
applications. The exact Steven Bartlett "high signal, low noise / high impact, low
apathy" wording was not verified in an accessible first-party source during this
research. It is useful as Giacomo's adopted strategy heuristic, but should not be
recorded as an empirical law or attributed verbatim to Bartlett without the original
source.
